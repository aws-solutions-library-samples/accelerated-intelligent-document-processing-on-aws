## Verdict

✅ **Ship, with three product findings a maintainer should read before the next release** —
the published artifact deploys in all five shipped configurations, both template transforms
deploy and process a real document, the upgrade from 0.6.11 applies without rollback, and the
342-run release A/B shows **0 regressions and 0 improvements**.

**7 of 8 tiers ran and passed. Tier G (browser UX) is the only tier NOT RUN** — no Chrome or
Chromium on the validation host, nothing on `127.0.0.1:9222`, and the `browsermcp` MCP server
disconnected mid-session rather than being absent. Tier H passed on one of its two suites:
`idp_common` is 19 of 19, while `idp_cli` selected 0 of 1,494 tests, which is an empty suite
rather than a pass (Finding 14).

**Tier F is complete.** F1, the release A/B, plus the F2–F4 guidance refresh: **29 scored
result sets — 3,248 scored rows from 2,488 runs, $1,970.55** — measured 2026-10-17 23:37 →
2026-10-18 06:22 UTC in one session on the upgraded reference stack, with all 28 lanes exiting
at their planned run count. The configuration matrix, both reference corpora, the scaling
study, the cost-variance runs, the two standing list-loss hazards, all nine extraction-model
arms, the classification and confidence sweeps and all three premium head-to-heads ran. Five
of §7's knob A/Bs and §2c's second model arm did not, and are named in Finding 5.

**The refresh produced the most consequential product finding in this record, and it is not a
regression in this release.** 17 of the 2,488 runs (0.68%) lost a document whose extraction
had already succeeded: in simple mode with `assessment: separate`, the separate confidence
pass exhausts its Lambda's memory or its 900 s wall clock *after* every row has been
extracted, validated, scored and persisted, and the whole document is then discarded
([#1488](https://github.com/aws-solutions-library-samples/accelerated-intelligent-document-processing-on-aws/issues/1488)).
This is the same defect shape as #1363, which this release fixed for the *integrated*
confidence path only. It is a configuration hazard rather than a blocker — advanced mode
completed every size in the scaling grid, and the integrated path is clean at 8/8 — but it is
marginal rather than rare at the sizes a customer is most likely to hit. See Finding 2.

**The tier-E finding is the one the aggregate numbers hide.** Diffing the
document processed before and after the upgrade gives 38 differing leaves of 1,852, while two
*same-version* control runs differ from each other on 56 of 1,860 — so by totals alone the
upgrade moved **less** than the model's own run-to-run variation, and "no change" is the
conclusion the numbers invite. Two groups of differences are nonetheless identical across all
three 0.6.12 runs and differ from 0.6.11, which is a version effect rather than noise: **six
Payslip statutory-tax amounts changed sign**, and the driver's-licence section populates one
field fewer. Neither is in the CHANGELOG. See Finding 1.

**Four results are improvements on 0.6.11, three of them closing items that record raised.**
Both standing list-loss hazards — `intconf` (integrated confidence with simple extraction) and
`advverify` — measure **clean at 8/8 with recall 1.000 and 100 of 100 rows on every run**,
which the v0.6.11 record could not say either way; the curated security snapshot now reads PASS
on all four gates where 0.6.11 published `SRT — FAIL ❌`; the `idp_common` integration suite
went from 1 failure of 19 to **19 of 19**; and the coverage registry grew from 9 trees to 11,
which moved the lowest-covered tree.

#### The 23 non-success runs mean three different things

0.92% of 2,488 runs did not succeed. **A single percentage is the wrong summary**, because the
23 behind it divide into a defect, a correct refusal, and an artefact of the measuring harness,
and only the first is a product problem:

| runs | share | where | attribution | what it means |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **17** | 0.68% | `large_narrow.pdf` ×15, `scale_1200` ×1, `scale_1600` ×1 | 15 `Runtime.OutOfMemory`, 2 `Sandbox.Timedout`, **all in `AssessmentStep` after `ExtractionStep` had succeeded** | the #1488 defect: a complete extraction, and the document discarded. Every one scores `completeness_recall` **1.000** with every row extracted — Finding 2 |
| **5** | 0.20% | `scale_3200.pdf` — 1 in `scaling`, 4 in `astracap` | `ExtractionInputTooLarge` | **by design and correct**: a named, up-front refusal with nothing attempted. In `astracap`, the 66-page ceiling study, this *is* the measured outcome rather than a failure of it |
| **1** | 0.04% | `coresynth__extraction-model-opus5` / `core-llm-simple-sep` / `large_narrow` | **not a product failure** — a harness polling timeout | scored `EXTRACTING`, `rows_extracted` 0, `wall_s` `None`, while the tracking table says that document reached `COMPLETED` / `SUCCEEDED`. The harness stopped waiting and recorded a non-success for a document that then succeeded |

Separately, **4 of the 760 (cell, corpus, document) triples on the reference corpora produced
no score at all** — `SCORE_ERROR` on `ocr_bench` in four different cells. That is a **scoring**
failure rather than a processing one: those documents processed and the scorer returned no
figure for them, which is why four cells read 19 of 20 scored rather than 20. It is counted in
neither the 23 above nor the accuracy figures below.

⚠️ **The memory failure is marginal, not deterministic, which is what makes it hard to see.**
`large_narrow.pdf` ran **318 executions across the refresh: 303 succeeded and 15 ran out of
memory**, about 5%. So it is a memory-pressure boundary at that document's size and shape, not
a size threshold — an 800-row document with a plainer layout completes every time, and a
customer hitting this sees intermittent loss rather than a reproducible limit.

#### Scaling: three regimes, and `completeness_recall` is not the signal to read

| rows | `core-tt-simple-sep` (simple mode) | `core-tt-adv-sep` (advanced mode) |
|---|---|---|
| 25, 100, 400, 800 | COMPLETED, recall 1.000 | COMPLETED, recall 1.000 |
| 1,200 | **FAILED** — `Sandbox.Timedout`; recall 1.000, 1,200/1,200 rows over 2,400 cells, all 1,200 confidence rows scored | COMPLETED, recall 1.000 |

built from recall alone reports simple mode as flawless at 1,600 rows. Advanced mode completed
every size in this grid, including 3,200 rows.

⚠️ **The denominators span 19% across the seven `coresynth` arms (70,406 to 83,790), so an
accuracy figure without its `cells_compared` says almost nothing.** A cell an arm never
returned is a cell it is not graded on, so an arm that returns fewer rows is scored on fewer
cells: 1.0000 over 73,570 cells (Sonnet 5.5) and 1.0000 over 83,790 (Opus 5.5) are not the same
achievement, and Nova Pro's 0.9361 over 6,960 cells against the control's 39,462 means "94%
accurate on the rows it chose to return". Recall is the column to rank on; accuracy is a
statement about the rows an arm did return.

**Claude Opus 5.5 is the only arm with both perfect recall and perfect cell accuracy**, and it
reaches them over the largest denominator of any arm, for $101.67 against GPT-6 Astra's
$209.28 on the same grid — **at least 51% less**, the bound rather than the figure because
Astra's cost is itself a lower bound (see below). Astra also returns every row but has the
lowest cell accuracy of the premium set (0.9737) over an essentially identical denominator, so
that is not an artefact of what it returned. Opus 5.5's three incomplete runs are the #1488
memory failure on one 17-page document, not a model property; Opus 5 and Sonnet 5.5 carry the
same cause for theirs.

⚠️ **Three of the cost figures are lower bounds, and this record quotes no cost *ratio* for
any of the three models.** `pricing.yaml` holds one flat rate per model and cannot express a
usage-dependent band: Haiku 5.5 is billed 5× above 100,000 input tokens and GPT-6 Astra moves
to a long-context band above 272,000, both crossed in practice here. Sonnet 5.5 is a different
problem with the same consequence — its published per-token prices are **identical to Sonnet
5's**, while the table prices Sonnet 5 at Sonnet 4.6's rates, so its $62.85 against Sonnet 5's
$86.10 is a **pricing-table artefact and not evidence that it is cheaper**. Compare those two
from the AWS price list, never from these figures.

a marginal improvement on the default at +5.8% cost, flagging nothing on this corpus.

### 1. Two reproducible, unannounced changes in extracted values across the upgrade — product, this release

`samples/lending_package.pdf` was processed four times: once on 0.6.11 before `update-stack`,
once on 0.6.12 after it, and **twice more on 0.6.12** as a same-version control. Timings,
`processing_report` free text and `confidence_reason` strings were excluded as noise.

| comparison | common leaves | differ | only-A | only-B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.6.11 vs 0.6.12-post | 1,852 | **38** | 20 | 2 |
| 0.6.12-ctrl1 vs 0.6.12-ctrl2 | 1,860 | **56** | 0 | 7 |
| 0.6.12-post vs 0.6.12-ctrl1 | 1,853 | 16 | 1 | 7 |
| 0.6.12-post vs 0.6.12-ctrl2 | 1,853 | 62 | 1 | 14 |

⚠️ **Read the totals and you get the wrong answer.** The upgrade diff (38) is *smaller* than
the same-version control diff (56), which says the upgrade moved less than the model's own
variation. Two groups of differences are nevertheless identical in **all three** 0.6.12 runs
and differ from 0.6.11 — agreement across repeats on one side is what separates a version
effect from noise, and a single run per side cannot see it.

**(a) Six Payslip statutory-tax amounts changed sign.**
`inference_result.{City,Federal,State}Taxes[*].Period`: 0.6.11 returns `'40.60'`, `'28.05'`,
`'6.56'`, `'8.43'`, `'5.94'`, `'0.60'`; every 0.6.12 run returns exactly those negated. The
field is declared `Period: "Current period amount for this federal tax item"` and evaluated
`NUMERIC_EXACT`, so the sign is scored rather than cosmetic.

Textract reads those lines as `- - 40.60`, `-6.56`, `- 8.43`, `- -5.94`, `- -0.60` under the
document's `Deductions / Statutory` heading, so the negative is arguably the more faithful
reading of the page. ⚠️ **That is inference from the OCR and the column heading, not a
measurement of correctness**: `samples/lending_package.pdf` has no ground-truth baseline in
the repository, so this record cannot say whether 0.6.12 corrected a long-standing error or
introduced one. Either way an operator comparing extracted tax figures across this upgrade
sees a sign change with nothing announcing it.

**(b) The driver's-licence section populates one field fewer.**
`metadata.population_check.fields_populated` 9 → 8 and `population_ratio` 0.818 → 0.727,
identically in all three 0.6.12 runs, because `ENDORSEMENTS` returns an empty list where
0.6.11 returned `['NONE']`. This also rewrites the `extraction_incomplete` processing issue to
name `ENDORSEMENTS, RESTRICTIONS` instead of `RESTRICTIONS` alone, and removes the
`metadata.coercion` block — 0.6.11 recorded a `type_family_mismatch` refusal on a null
`RESTRICTIONS` that 0.6.12 does not produce.

**A contrast that validates the method:** section 3's `fields_populated` also moves (0.6.11
and 0.6.12-post both 8, both controls 9). Same shape as the two above, but it fails the
agreement test, so that one **is** nondeterminism.

**Mechanism not isolated.** `config_library/` is unchanged between the tags apart from
`pricing.yaml`, `model_config_limits.yaml` and `extensions-marketplace.yaml` — nothing in the
lending-package preset — so the configuration is identical on both sides and the cause is in
code. `lib/idp_common_pkg/idp_common/extraction/service.py` gained 367 lines this release and
nothing in the diff mentions sign, negation or absolute value. **Neither change appears in the
0.6.12 CHANGELOG.** Both deserve either a changelog entry or a fix.

### 2. The separate confidence pass discards a fully extracted document on large inputs — product, pre-existing, now measured

**17 of the refresh's 2,488 runs (0.68%) threw away a document whose extraction had already
succeeded.** Fifteen were `Runtime.OutOfMemory` and two `Sandbox.Timedout`, and all seventeen
failed in the same place. Step Functions history for one of each — `large_narrow` for the
memory failure, `scale_1200` for the timeout — shows an identical sequence: `ExtractionStep`
**exits successfully**, `PostExtractionHook` succeeds, `AssessmentStep` is entered, its Lambda
fails (once retried), then `MapIterationFailed` → `MapStateFailed` → `ExecutionFailed`. So in
simple mode with `assessment: separate`, the separate confidence pass is a single point of
failure on large documents, and it fails **after** every row has been extracted, validated,
confidence-scored and persisted to S3. Two different resource limits — the 900 s Lambda wall
clock and Lambda memory — in one step. Filed as
[#1488](https://github.com/aws-solutions-library-samples/accelerated-intelligent-document-processing-on-aws/issues/1488).

**This is the same defect shape as #1363, which this release fixed for the *integrated*
confidence path only.** The separate path still discards the document. The 25-row default
confidence batch cap is part of the mechanism: 1,600 rows is roughly 64 serial scoring calls
inside one 900 s invocation.

⚠️ **The failure is invisible in the accuracy metrics, which is the part to carry forward.**
Every one of the seventeen scores `completeness_recall` **1.000** with every row present,
because the harness reads extraction output from S3 and that output is complete. A table built
from recall rather than from `status` reports this configuration as flawless at 1,600 rows.

**It is not a row threshold, and it is not deterministic.** `scale_800` (800 rows) completes in
simple mode, while `large_narrow` — also 800 rows, but 17 pages and about 203,000 estimated
input tokens — ran **318 executions across the refresh and lost 15 of them, about 5%**, in
three different cells. So the boundary is a resource limit at a particular size and shape, and
a customer meeting it sees intermittent loss rather than a reproducible ceiling. It sits at
exactly the document sizes a customer is most likely to hit.

**Two configurations measured clean against it on this build**, which is the practical remedy
until the defect is fixed: advanced mode completed every size in the scaling grid including
3,200 rows, and the integrated confidence path (`intconf`) is 8/8 with recall 1.000 and 100 of
100 rows on every run.

### 3. Claude Sonnet 5.5, new and selectable in this release, loses almost the whole list on 400-row documents — product, this release

**13 of its 133 `coresynth` runs return 2 or 10 rows of a 400-row list**, and they are not
marginal losses:

| recall | rows returned | documents / cells | `cells_compared` | cell accuracy on those runs |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 0.005 | **2 of 400** | `med_narrow` × 4 cells | *none recorded* | *not scored* |
| 0.025 | **10 of 400** | `med_narrow`, `wide_400`, `manylists_400` × 9 cells | 20 | 1.000 |

⚠️ **Its headline cell accuracy of 1.0000 is computed over the 20 cells it returned.** The arm's
mean recall of 0.9041 over 73,570 compared cells — among the three smallest denominators in the
grid — is a bimodal distribution read as an average: every one of the 13 is a **400-row** document and the
smaller documents in the grid are unaffected, which is what makes a mean of 0.9041 look
survivable. On a 400-row list this is the worst arm in the grid, not the cheapest good one.

**Detection is mostly present and fails at the extreme.** 9 of the 13 carry
`extraction_rows_below_ocr_estimate`. The four worst — the 2-of-400 runs — carry only
`extraction_validation_failed` on three of them and **no completeness issue at all** on the
fourth, and the coverage walk recorded no denominator for any of the four, so the coverage rung
could not speak either. Those same four runs are what falsified a pinned test premise
(Finding 8).

Three further properties make the model's other columns non-comparable, independent of the row
loss: it **rejects a forced `toolChoice`**, so Schema Enforcement falls back to the prose
schema and `extraction.forced_tool` is recorded as skipped; it rejects
`temperature`/`top_p`/`top_k`, which the accelerator strips for this model id; and **1M is its
default window with no `:1m` id**, which makes the derived agentic shard budget 578,400 tokens
against Sonnet 5's 18,400 — wide shards defeat prompt caching, so its cost per document is not
comparable on the agentic path unless `extraction.agentic.shard_token_budget` is pinned.

Filed as
[#1494](https://github.com/aws-solutions-library-samples/accelerated-intelligent-document-processing-on-aws/issues/1494)
and **parked pending a product decision** — whether to keep the model selectable as is, pin its
shard budget, or gate it by document size, is a product call rather than a validation one. The
guidance half has shipped: the configuration paper's model section carries the measurement and
recommends against selecting it for list extraction on documents of a few hundred rows or more.

### 4. On a real labelled corpus, simple mode beats advanced — at half the cost — configuration guidance, this release

This is the result the synthetic grid **cannot** produce, and the reason running the reference
corpora matters more than their row count suggests. The 19 synthetic cells score 1.000 on recall
and cell accuracy almost everywhere, so that grid ranks nothing. RealKIE-FCC-Verified ranks
them, and it inverts the intuition that the agentic path is the more capable one:

| RealKIE cell | weighted accuracy | cost/doc | wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| `split-disabled` | **0.812** | $0.1251 | 44 s |
| `core-tt-simple-sep` | 0.807 | $0.1423 | 38 s |
| `core-tt-simple-off` | 0.805 | $0.1392 | 29 s |
| `core-tl-adv-sep` | 0.791 | $0.2464 | 53 s |
| `core-tt-adv-int` | 0.768 | $0.4870 | 63 s |
| `core-bda-adv-sep` | 0.760 | $0.3142 | 53 s |
| `core-tt-adv-sep` | **0.758** | $0.2757 | 52 s |

**The three best cells are all simple mode and the three worst are all advanced**, and cost runs
about 2× in the same direction (`core-tt-simple-sep` $0.1423 against `core-tt-adv-sep` $0.2757).
On this corpus advanced extraction pays more for less.

Three bounds on that. The figures are `weighted_accuracy` from the **product's own evaluation
against the corpus labels**, so there is no `cells_compared` to quote — the metric is absent on
these rows by construction, and the denominator to read them against is the **756 scored rows
over 760 (cell, corpus, document) triples** with 0 parse failures. Only the **control** arm ran,
so this is a cross-configuration result on this release and **not** a model comparison. And
RealKIE's documents are not long lists, so this says nothing about completeness at scale, which
is the scaling study's territory and where advanced mode is unambiguously better: it completed
every size in that grid, while simple mode is the configuration that loses whole documents
(Finding 2). **So this is a recommendation about labelled real-world forms, not a general one**
— on long lists the ranking reverses.

### 5. Five §7 knob A/Bs and §2c's second model arm were not re-measured — process, this pass

Tier F is complete and the guidance papers are refreshed on this build; what is left unmeasured
is narrow, and these are the figures not to cite as v0.6.12 results:

- **Five of §7's knob A/Bs did not run** — `splitcost`, `advsplitcost`, `boundaryab`,
  `multiinstance` and `sizerab`. Those subsections keep figures from earlier editions and say
  so in their first sentence. `enforcement`, `enforcecost`, `forcing` and the schema-restatement
  arms inside `core` were re-measured.
- **§2c ran at the control model only**, so the *two-model* comparison the previous editions
  published is not refreshed. Comparing a v0.6.12 control figure against a v0.6.8
  shipped-default figure would be a cross-release difference wearing a model label, so §2c is
  a cross-configuration result on this release and the v0.6.8 pair remains the only
  like-for-like model comparison on those corpora.

One metric is missing from every table in the refresh, and it is a gap rather than a choice:
**`typed_accuracy` is emitted as `null` on all 3,248 scored rows**
([#1490](https://github.com/aws-solutions-library-samples/accelerated-intelligent-document-processing-on-aws/issues/1490)),
so no per-model typed-accuracy column could be produced. It is the one metric that separates a
value extracted with the right *type* from one that merely matches as a string.

### 6. The lowest-covered tree changed, and one tree lost coverage inside the ratchet's tolerance — gate, pre-existing

The coverage registry grew from 9 trees to 11: `src_lambda` (89.90%) and **`api_resolvers`
(71.01%)** are new since the v0.6.11 record. `api_resolvers` is now the lowest-covered tree by
11 points, displacing `seller_entitlement` (82.03%), which the previous record named as the one
to watch. `api_resolvers` holds the UI API resolver Lambdas — the layer this release moved
*every document read* behind (#1033) — so it is the tree where low coverage costs most right
now.

Separately, **`main_stack_extensions` measured 93.86% against the previous record's 95.64%, a
loss of 1.78 points**, which the per-file ratchet's 1% tolerance absorbed, so
`make check-coverage-debt` stayed green. That is the ratchet behaving as designed and is
exactly why the release record carries the measured per-tree table and its delta: a tree can
lose coverage with every gate green.

### 7. The assessment coverage WARNING rung has no absolute ceiling, so its silent band grows with list length — gate, pre-existing

`benchmarks/tests/test_coverage_threshold_evidence.py` pins the largest number of unscored rows
recorded on a run the coverage **warning** rung said nothing about. Re-measured on the v0.6.12
grids it is **115 unscored rows of 3,200 (3.6%)** on `scale_3200.pdf` / `astra-adv-wide`,
against **24 of 800 (3.0%)** when it was last measured.

⚠️ **The two measurements together are the finding, not the new number.** The row count grew
4.8× while the *share* barely moved, because the document grew 4× — which is exactly what a
purely multiplicative rung does. The silent band is proportional to list length, so the
absolute number of rows that can go unreported has no bound at all; it is 115 here only because
3,200 rows is the longest list in the corpus, and a longer production list carries a
proportionally larger band. That is the same shape #1361 fixed for
`extraction_rows_below_ocr_estimate`, by replacing a multiplicative tolerance with a 29-row
additive one, and it is the argument for giving this warning rung an absolute ceiling the way
its error rung already carries an absolute floor. Tracked as
[#1495](https://github.com/aws-solutions-library-samples/accelerated-intelligent-document-processing-on-aws/issues/1495).
The pin only stops the figure growing unnoticed again; a pin over a committed corpus tracks the
longest list anyone benchmarked rather than bounding production.

### 8. Committing the result sets moved three pinned premises in `benchmarks/tests`, and one of them was falsified — test, this pass

Those suites assert over the committed result sets, so landing 29 of them re-measures their
premises rather than merely adding data. Three moved, and the distinction between the first two
and the third is the point:

- **`MAX_SILENT_UNSCORED_ROWS` 24 → 115.** A re-measurement of the same quantity on a longer
  document — Finding 7.
- **The exact model-axis count, 19 → 20**, in `benchmarks/tests/test_model_axis_coverage.py`,
  because `sonnet55` was added to the `extraction_model` axis and its sweep list. The count is
  asserted exactly so that a model *disappearing* from an axis fails rather than passing
  quietly, which means it is meant to be updated deliberately in the same change.
- ⚠️ **`test_an_undefined_coverage_run_extracted_nothing` was falsified, not moved.** Its
  premise is that a run reporting no coverage denominator extracted nothing — the guard
  declines to fire when a section produced no list row. Four
  `coresynth__extraction-model-sonnet55` runs on `med_narrow.pdf` extracted **2 rows of 400**
  while recording no denominator, so the coverage rung could not speak about the 398 that were
  lost. That is #1494's coverage-walk half (Finding 3), not a reason to relax the check, so the
  four are carved out **by name and by count** — that grid and that document, exactly four — and
  a fifth instance, or the same shape on another model or document, fails there. If the count
  drops to zero the defect is fixed and the carve-out is deleted with it.

A premise that is false and merely relaxed is how a check becomes decoration; bounding the
carve-out on both axes is what keeps the next instance visible.

### 9. SRT's open-finding count rose 7 → 10 — test code, explainable

All ten are Bandit `B105`/`B106`: matches of an *identifier's name* against a password
wordlist, in test files no deployment artifact is built from, reported at Bandit's own severity
rather than blocking. The three new ones are `test_delete_documents_selector_failure.py`,
`test_delete_documents_coverage.py` and `test_batch_delete_selector_failure.py` — test files
this release added with the document-deletion fix (#1366). Nothing reopened and the
suppression register is non-vacuous at 52 entries. No action; recorded because any movement in
a gate value is a finding.

### 10. Tier G could not run, and this is the release where that costs most — environment

No Chrome or Chromium on the validation host, nothing listening on `127.0.0.1:9222`, no
`chrome-devtools` MCP, and `browsermcp` **disconnected during the session** rather than being
absent. This is the second consecutive release with tier G NOT RUN.

It matters more here than at 0.6.11 because this release's UI surface is large: page rotation
in four editors, the "why this field has no bounding box" control on all four field-displaying
surfaces, Test Studio's server-side document search, the annotation-queue advance, the
prefix-mapping admin screens, the malware-scan banner and detail field, and the cost panel's
unpriced-unit handling. The compensating coverage is the **UI unit suite — 124 files, 1,507
tests, all passing** — which runs in neither `make test` nor `make lint-cicd` and was invoked
separately. **Unit coverage is not a person completing a flow**, and no flow in
`scripts/ux_flows.yaml` was exercised.

### 11. A test writes build output into the tree, which silently degrades the next SRT scan — test tooling, pre-existing

`lib/idp_sdk/tests/unit/test_multi_doc_discovery_source_zip.py` writes a ~7 MB
`.aws-sam/multi-doc-discovery-source.zip` into the **repository root** while it runs. Any
`make srt-scan` ordered after `make test` therefore reports `1 .aws-sam build directory
present` and drops into its degraded mode, emitting phantom LOCAL-ONLY findings and risking a
checkov crash on the largest templates.

The scan segregated them correctly, so the gate verdict held — but the preflight rule in
`.claude/skills/release-validation.md` is "scan before `publish.py`", and that is not
sufficient: it has to be before `make test` too. Either the test should write to a temporary
directory, or the preflight rule should say so.

### 12. `ruff` on PATH is not the pinned version, and the warning is easy to pass over — gate, environment

`make lint-cicd` printed `⚠️ ruff 0.15.20 is first on PATH, but this repo pins ruff 0.15.13`
and then **passed**. Findings and formatting are both properties of the linter version, so a
green run under the wrong one does not establish the CI result. Re-run under the pinned
version via a shim (`uvx ruff@0.15.13`): `make ruff-lint check-lint-debt` exits 0, `ruff check
--fix` modified nothing, and the lint-debt gate reports 82 files excluded from `ruff check`
and 171 from `ruff format`, all carrying exactly their recorded findings. So the gate is
genuinely green — but it was green for the wrong reason until re-run, and the warning is one
line in a long log.

### 13. RBAC dynamic grew 609 → 876 checks; 17 are skipped and a skipped check asserted nothing — test, informational

Movement is in the expected direction and consistent with the release's new API surface
(`getFilePresignedUrls`, the prefix-mapping operations, the malware-scan fields). The

moved 15 → 17 and sit in three suites: 11 in the positive authorization matrix, 3 in
config-version scope enforcement, 1 in token lifecycle (with its 1 known-gap warning), 2 in

matters — a check that could not run establishes nothing.

### 14. A defect open across two releases has closed — product, improvement

`idp_common`'s integration suite is **19 passed, 0 failed, 0 skipped** (11,859 deselected). At
v0.6.11 it was 1 failure of 19, and that failure reproduced identically at v0.6.10. This is the

### 15. The security snapshot's SRT gate stopped contradicting the live scan — gate, improvement

### 16. Four orphaned ENIs in the stack-test VPC, two of them not from this run — environment
